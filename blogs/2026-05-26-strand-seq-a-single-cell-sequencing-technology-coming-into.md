How advances in automation, single cell biology, and next-generation sequencing are finally unlocking the full promise of one of genomics' most powerful, and most underutilized, techniques. The problem with knowing half a genome You carry two copies of every chromosome, one from each parent, and those copies can differ in ways that matter enormously for disease risk and drug response. Assembling both copies end to end, and determining which variant came from which parent, is the true goal of personalized genomics.
